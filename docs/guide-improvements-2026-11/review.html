Important before/after excerpts

Do Motor Dealers Need FCA Authorisation? — answer

Before

Selling cars does not, by itself, mean a dealership needs FCA authorisation. Introducing customers to regulated credit or consumer hire can bring the business within FCA regulation. Check the activities you actually carry out, the permissions they require and whether you will operate through direct authorisation or an appropriate appointed-representative arrangement.

After

Usually, yes, if you introduce customers to regulated car finance: you need the relevant Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) authorisation or a valid appointed-representative arrangement covering that activity. Selling vehicles for cash alone does not require credit-broking permission. Check the actual activities and any applicable exclusions before starting; using another finance broker does not automatically remove your own responsibilities.

Limited Permission FCA Authorisation for Motor Dealers — answer

Before

Limited permission can be appropriate for some motor dealers, but it is not determined simply by the size of the dealership or by calling finance a secondary service. Vehicle ownership, the agreements introduced, sourcing activities and part-exchange finance settlement can change which permissions are needed.

After

A motor dealer selling its own vehicles and introducing buyers to third-party finance may qualify for limited-permission secondary credit broking. That is a starting point, not a rule for every dealer. The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) route depends on stock ownership, agreement types, sourcing, part-exchanges and other regulated activities—not simply the size of the business.

Why FCA Applications Get Delayed: A Motor Dealer Guide — faqs.0.answer

Before

No average is established by this guide. The FCA publishes assessment timescales of usually within six months for a complete FSMA application and up to twelve months for an incomplete one.

After

No. Six and twelve months describe formal assessment periods, not an average approval time. The FCA separately publishes a 95% four-/ten-month operating target for complete/incomplete new Part 4A applications. Neither means that an individual firm is guaranteed approval.

Before

This practical checklist is designed to help organise that work. It does not replace the current FCA form, completion notes or your firm’s submission deadline.

Use this CCR009 checklist when the draft return and supporting records are ready for final review. Check the firm, period, applicable questions, reconciliations, unresolved items and submitter access; then confirm the return was actually submitted. It is a practical readiness aid, not the FCA form or a replacement for your firm’s deadline.

Remaining issues and scope limits

  • Official-source conflict: exactly £5 million and first-period overlaps need FCA/qualified confirmation for affected firms. The visible caveat is deliberate.
  • Outside guide scope: the FCA service page’s older timing explanation was not amended. Pricing-page reporting copy contains an existing price-bearing paragraph outside the campaign fallback guard; no non-guide pricing correction was authorised in this task.
  • Commercial unknowns: VAT and cancellation notice are still null in approved configuration; no value was invented or commercial term changed.
  • Measurement: legitimate consent integration and booking-start/completion instrumentation require separate shared/booking-flow work. Search Console query/page exports would help test overlap and prioritise subsequent changes; live rankings, CTR, bookings and field Core Web Vitals have not been measured after deployment because nothing is deployed.
  • Research limits: observed results are a small UK-focused sample, not a controlled geolocated Google ranking study. No keyword volumes or causal gains inferred.
  • Review scope: revised material is evidence-checked; unchanged guides retain original source-check dates. This is not a certification of every possible application circumstance or all future regulatory changes.
